## User Personas

### Persona 1: Alex Chen

![Alex Chen](./docs/imgs/Alex-chen.jpg)

**Age:** 28  
**Location:** Portland, Oregon  
**Occupation:** Software Developer  
**Tech Comfort Level:** High

#### Background
Alex works remotely as a software developer and spends most of his day in front of a computer. On weekends, he seeks outdoor activities to decompress and stay active. He grew up playing video games and still enjoys competitive gaming experiences like Pokémon GO and geocaching apps.

#### Goals
- Find a fun, gamified way to explore nature trails near Portland
- Compete with friends on leaderboards and earn achievements
- Learn about local wildlife without needing to carry field guides

#### Frustrations
- Most nature apps feel boring or too educational without any engagement hooks
- Wants something that combines his love of gaming with outdoor exploration
- Hates apps that require constant internet connectivity on remote trails

#### How He Uses the App
Alex uses the Competitive Wildlife Scavenger App primarily for the **leaderboard and achievement system**. He organizes weekend challenges with his hiking group, competing to photograph the rarest wildlife. He appreciates the **point system and rarity multipliers** that reward finding uncommon species.

### Persona 2: Dr. Patricia Morales

![Patricia Morales](./docs/imgs/patricia-morales.jpg)

**Age:** 45  
**Location:** Eugene, Oregon  
**Occupation:** Middle School Science Teacher  
**Tech Comfort Level:** Moderate

#### Background
Patricia has been teaching life sciences for 18 years and is always looking for ways to make learning engaging for her students. She leads the school's nature club and organizes monthly field trips to local parks and wildlife refuges. She's comfortable with educational technology but prefers apps that are intuitive and don't require extensive setup.

#### Goals
- Find interactive tools to teach students about local ecosystems
- Create structured outdoor activities that keep students engaged
- Track student participation and learning outcomes

#### Frustrations
- Many wildlife apps are designed for adults and overwhelm younger users
- Difficult to find apps that work well for group educational settings
- Wants accountability features to ensure students are actually participating

#### How She Uses the App
Patricia uses the app to create **missions and challenges** for her nature club field trips. She sets up scavenger hunts where students must photograph and identify specific plants and animals. The **Anidex feature** serves as a digital field guide her students can reference, and the **friend system** allows her to monitor which students have completed their challenges.

### Persona 3: James Morgan "Saul Goodman" McGill

![James McGill](./docs/img/jimmy-mcgill.jpg)

**Age:** 67  
**Location:** Bend, Oregon  
**Occupation:** Retired Park Ranger  
**Tech Comfort Level:** Low to Moderate

#### Background
Bobby spent 35 years as a park ranger at various Oregon state parks before retiring. He has extensive knowledge of Pacific Northwest wildlife and now volunteers with local conservation groups. He recently got a smartphone to stay connected with his grandchildren and is slowly learning to use apps. He's an avid birdwatcher and wildlife photographer who keeps detailed journals of his sightings.

#### Goals
- Document and catalog wildlife sightings digitally instead of paper journals
- Share his knowledge and discoveries with the conservation community
- Stay mentally active and learn new technology at his own pace

#### Frustrations
- Many apps have small text and complicated interfaces
- Feels intimidated by apps with too many features
- Wants to contribute his expertise but doesn't know how

#### How He Uses the App
Bobby primarily uses the **User Photo Identification feature** to document birds and animals he spots on his daily walks. He appreciates that the AI can help confirm his identifications. The **Anidex** serves as a digital catalog of everything he's seen, replacing his handwritten journals. He occasionally participates in community challenges but mainly enjoys the personal cataloging features and the **map display** showing where he's made discoveries over time.

### Persona 4: Lily Yates

![Lily Yates](./docs/imgs/lily-yates.jpg)

**Age:** 23  
**Location:** Corvallis, Oregon  
**Occupation:** Senior at Oregon State University, Fish and Wildlife Major  
**Tech Comfort Level:** High

#### Background
Lily is finishing her senior year at OSU studying Fish and Wildlife Sciences. She spends a lot of time doing fieldwork for her classes and undergraduate research, often needing to document species sightings for lab reports and research projects. She's passionate about conservation and hopes to work for Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife after graduation. She's active on social media and enjoys sharing her wildlife photography with friends.

#### Goals
- Quickly document and identify species during fieldwork without carrying heavy field guides
- Build a personal database of sightings for academic projects and her portfolio
- Share discoveries with classmates and connect with other wildlife enthusiasts

#### Frustrations
- Current field documentation methods are tedious and time-consuming
- Wants an app that can help with quick ID confirmations when she's unsure about a species
- Needs something that works offline in remote field locations

#### How She Uses the App
Lily uses the **User Photo Identification feature** extensively during fieldwork to get quick AI-assisted confirmations on species she photographs. The **Anidex** helps her learn about species outside her specialty area (she focuses on fish but wants to expand her knowledge of birds and mammals). She enjoys the **friend system** to connect with classmates and share interesting finds, and uses the **map display** to track where she's documented species for her research notes. The competitive elements are fun for her, but she's primarily motivated by the educational and documentation features.
